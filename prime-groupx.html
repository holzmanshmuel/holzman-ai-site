The problem

Skilled work, done by hand, every day.

Prime's operations ran on skilled manual work. The work was good business, but nearly every recurring task was done by hand: detailed, repeatable and never finished. The firm could only grow as fast as the team could add hours.

What we built

The busywork runs on its own. People check where it matters.

The repetitive work, automated

The detailed, do-it-again-tomorrow tasks that filled the team's days now run on their own, with a person checking the result where it matters.

Every tool behind one sign-in

Staff open one page, sign in once with their company account, and see the tools they're allowed to use. The CEO adds or removes people himself. It replaced shared passwords and a scatter of separate web addresses.

Training by video, not by the CEO

Each tool comes with a 90-second narrated video and a written how-to, so a new team member learns it without anyone walking them through it live.

When something breaks, we hear first

Every automation retries on its own and alerts us when it fails. The team sees a plain-English message, never a raw error, and doesn't have to debug anything.

The result

Time back for the work only people can do.

The first automation went live in March 2026 and is still in use seven months later. The team spends its day on clients and decisions instead of the steps a system can repeat.

The work hasn't stopped at launch. When a new bottleneck shows up, we automate that too, and every automation is monitored after it ships.
